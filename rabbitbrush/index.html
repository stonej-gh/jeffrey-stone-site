The app

iPhone beta

Rabbitbrush: a plant identifier for the western US, on your phone

Take a photo of a plant, or pick one from your library, and get a ranked list of five species, each with its common name and a Wikipedia link. The classifier runs on the iPhone's Neural Engine in about a quarter of a second a photo. Nothing is uploaded to identify a photo.

What you get

Knows

4,187 species, eleven states

The list is the species iNaturalist observers have recorded in Colorado, Wyoming, Montana, Idaho, Utah, Nevada, New Mexico, Arizona, Washington, Oregon and California, wild and cultivated plants both. The classifier is a DINOv2 vision transformer I fine-tuned at home on one GPU.

For

Someone in the western US with a plant in front of them

Outside those states the list is the wrong list. The app has no class for your plant, so it will be confidently wrong, and nothing you send back would teach it anything. I would rather say that here than have you find out over a bad week.

Unsure

It says when it is not sure

When the top answer is not clearly ahead the app says so instead of pretending. Two states: the genus looks right but not the species, or nothing close. Either way it asks for a close-up of the part that would settle it, a flower, a leaf, bark, a cone.

Location

Optional, and it stays on the phone

With Settings > Location on, wild species are re-ranked by what is recorded within about 100 km of the photo: the phone's position for a new shot, the photo's own GPS for one from your library. The table that does it ships inside the app, so the location is not sent anywhere to look it up. Off by default.

Why a beta

The testers are teaching the model.

Under every result there is a field check: which match was right, or the plant's name when the list missed it, and a note. The app keeps these on your phone. If you agree to share them, each one comes to me as you make it, and the misses are the list the next training round has to answer. A test set tells me how the model does on photos like the ones it trained on. You tell me how it does in the hand of someone standing in front of a plant. What sharing sends, and what it never sends, is written out in full.
