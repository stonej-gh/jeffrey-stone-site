Privacy

Spacewar collects nothing unless you share a score

Spacewar: Orbital Duel runs on your device. It uses no analytics, no advertising and no accounts. The one thing it can send is a high score, and only after you turn sharing on.

What the game keeps on your device

Your settings, the high score table (three initials, a score and the level reached for each run), a run in progress, and a random id made on your device for the score board. None of it leaves the device unless you share.

What sharing sends, if you turn it on

The HIGH SCORES screen has a WORLD view: every player's shared runs, from iPhones and Android devices alike, kept on a server the developer runs. Its SHARE MY SCORES button is off until you tap it. With sharing on, each run on your table is sent once, over an encrypted connection: your initials, the score, the level reached, whether you played on a phone or a tablet, the game's version, and the random id of this install. Nothing else about you: no name, no account, no location, no contacts. Opening the WORLD view sends the install id alone, so the game can show your own rows in gold and your place among the shared runs.

Initials the developer judges offensive are shown on the board as three dashes; the score keeps its place.

Your control

Tap the sharing button again to stop. Stopping removes every run from this install from the board, and an UNDO shows for a few seconds if you change your mind. Clearing the table on your device does not touch the board. If you delete and reinstall the game, the new install has a new id, and runs shared by the old one stay on the board; to have those removed, write to support with the initials, score and level, and they will be taken down.

The App Store privacy label

Data collected, not linked to your identity: Gameplay Content (the shared runs), Identifiers (the random install id). Purpose: App Functionality. Not used for tracking. Nothing is collected unless sharing is on.

The Google Play data safety form

Data collected, optional, not shared with third parties, not linked to your identity: Other user-generated content (the initials, score and level of a shared run), Device or other IDs (the random install id). Purpose: app functionality. Encrypted in transit; removed when you stop sharing, or on request. Nothing is collected unless sharing is on.

The App Store and Google Play

If Apple's TestFlight and App Store, or Google Play, collect anything as part of distributing the game, that is governed by Apple's privacy policy or Google's privacy policy, not this one.

Standing on shoulders

A modern tribute to Spacewar!, created in 1962 by Steve Russell and friends on MIT's PDP‑1, one of the first video games ever made. Sixty years on, the pull of the star still decides the duel.
